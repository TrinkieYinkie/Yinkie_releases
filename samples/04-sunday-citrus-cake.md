# Sunday Citrus Olive-Oil Cake

*Bright at the edges, tender in the middle, and forgiving enough for a slow
morning.*

> [!TIP]
> Read the whole recipe once, then leave the butter in the refrigerator. This
> cake gets its soft crumb from olive oil.

## At a glance

| Makes | Preparation | Baking | Resting |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| One 23 cm cake | 20 minutes | 40–48 minutes | 30 minutes |

## Ingredients

### For the cake

| Ingredient | Amount | Note |
| :--- | ---: | :--- |
| Plain flour | 210 g | Spoon and level |
| Fine sugar | 180 g | Plus a little for the top |
| Baking powder | 2 tsp | Fresh, if possible |
| Fine sea salt | ½ tsp | |
| Eggs | 3 large | Room temperature |
| Extra-virgin olive oil | 160 ml | Fruity, not aggressively bitter |
| Plain yogurt | 140 g | Full-fat gives the softest crumb |
| Orange | 1 | Zest and 60 ml juice |
| Lemon | 1 | Zest only |
| Vanilla extract | 1 tsp | |

### For finishing

- 2 tbsp orange juice
- 2 tbsp fine sugar
- a small pinch of flaky salt
- optional: softly whipped yogurt or crème fraîche

## Mise en place

- [ ] Heat the oven to **175°C**.
- [ ] Line the base of a 23 cm round tin.
- [ ] Zest the citrus directly over the measured sugar.
- [ ] Bring the eggs and yogurt to room temperature.
- [ ] Measure the finishing juice before clearing the board.

## Method

1. **Wake up the citrus.** Rub the orange and lemon zest into the sugar with
   your fingertips until the sugar feels damp and smells vivid.
2. **Mix the dry ingredients.** Whisk the flour, baking powder, and salt in a
   medium bowl.
3. **Build the batter.** In a larger bowl, whisk the citrus sugar and eggs for
   about one minute. Add the olive oil in a thin stream, then whisk in the
   yogurt, orange juice, and vanilla.
4. **Fold gently.** Add the dry ingredients and fold just until no pockets of
   flour remain. The goal is a tender crumb, not ~~perfection~~ symmetry.
5. **Bake.** Pour into the tin, smooth the top, and scatter over a teaspoon of
   sugar. Bake until deeply golden and a tester comes out with a few moist
   crumbs, **40–48 minutes**.
6. **Finish while warm.** Stir the orange juice with the sugar and flaky salt.
   Spoon it slowly over the cake after it has rested for ten minutes.
7. **Wait.** Let the cake settle before slicing. The crumb becomes silkier as it
   cools.

> [!CAUTION]
> If the center still moves like liquid when the tin is nudged, keep baking in
> five-minute increments. A dark top is welcome; a raw center is not.

## What “done” looks like

- The top is burnished and slightly crackled.
- The edges pull away from the tin by a millimeter or two.
- A tester carries moist crumbs but no wet batter.
- The kitchen smells more like orange peel than sugar.

<details>
<summary>Substitutions and variations</summary>

### Grapefruit

Replace the orange zest with grapefruit zest, but keep orange or mandarin juice
for a rounder finish.

### Almond

Replace 40 g of the flour with finely ground almonds. Expect a slightly denser,
moister slice.

### Dairy-free

Use a thick, unsweetened plant-based yogurt. Choose one with a neutral flavor
and enough body to sit on a spoon.

### A quieter finish

Skip the syrup and dust the cooled cake with icing sugar just before serving.

</details>

## Serving note

This cake is best cut into generous wedges and eaten from small plates. Serve it
plain in the afternoon, or add a spoonful of softly whipped yogurt after
dinner. The flavor is even better on the second day.[^storage]

> A good everyday cake does not ask for an occasion. It creates one.

[^storage]: Keep the cooled cake covered at room temperature for up to two days,
    or refrigerate it for longer storage and let each slice lose its chill
    before serving.
